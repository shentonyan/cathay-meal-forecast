# Industry Benchmarks and Airline Case Studies

> Research notes behind the [report](../../docs/research.html), one per research track. Hover over any source link to see the supporting quote from that source.

## 1. Industry benchmark data

**Cabin waste per passenger**
- IATA/ASF ran 25 audits and found an average of 0.94 kg of waste per passenger, 3.6 million tonnes in total in 2023 [IATA Sustainable Cabin](https://www.iata.org/policy/environment/pages/cabin-waste.aspx "the sector generated an average of 0.94 kg of waste per passenger, which translates into 3.6 million tonnes of cabin waste in 2023"). At current passenger growth, this volume will double by 2040 [IATA Sustainable Cabin](https://www.iata.org/policy/environment/pages/cabin-waste.aspx "With current passenger growth rates, this waste volume is set to double by 2040.").
- Per flight, the average is 237.3 kg [IATA Chart of the Week](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/airline-cabin-waste--progress-toward-a-circular-economy/ "waste of 237.3 kilograms per flight or 0.94 kilograms per passenger.").
- An earlier measure (a Heathrow study) put waste at about 1.43 kg per passenger per flight, close to 6 million tonnes a year, of which about 20% was untouched food and drink [IATA Airlines Magazine](https://www.airlines.iata.org/analysis/rule-changes-are-needed-to-deliver-a-sustainable-cabin "The average passenger generates approximately 1.43kg of waste per flight, equating to nearly 6 million tonnes of waste per year... Approximately 20% of this is untouched food and drink."). IATA figures show 6.1 million tonnes of cabin waste globally in 2018 [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/lifestyle/travel-leisure/article/3116767/airline-food-waste-no-meal-option-ai-identify-uneaten "airlines generated 6.1 million tonnes of cabin waste globally in 2018").

**Share and value of untouched food**
- The latest IATA fact sheet says 34% of cabin waste is untouched food and beverages, so around US$6 billion of resources is incinerated or landfilled each year [IATA Fact Sheet Cabin Waste](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-cabin-waste/ "34% of cabin waste is untouched food and beverages, representing around $6 billion of resources incinerated or landfilled each year.").
- Preliminary 2024 ASF audits: 65% of waste was food and beverage waste, and untouched meals were 18% of all waste [IATA Press Release 2024-07-04](https://iata.org/en/pressroom/2024-releases/2024-07-04-01 "with 65% being food and beverage waste. Untouched meals account for 18% of all waste.").
- An earlier estimate: the inflight catering market is worth US$20 billion and 20% goes untouched, about US$4 billion [IATA GMD 2022](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/f9f1f10a29524cf68326605e9982bb8d/sustainable-cabin-jon-godson-olga-miltcheva-gmd2022.pdf "with an inflight catering market of $20 billion(a), the sector is currently incinerating and landfilling $4 billion worth of food & drink.").
- At an untouched share of 18–20%, that is about 700,000 metric tonnes a year [Aeronautics Magazine](https://aeronauticsmagazine.com/featured/airlines-could-save-millions-per-year-by-letting-ai-decide-how-much-food-to-load-airbus-says "with 18% to 20% of all catering materials returning completely untouched — roughly 772,000 tons (700,000 metric tonnes) annually.").
- Cost per meal by cabin: no reliable public figures were found in this search. Cathay internal data or gategroup/LSG quotes would be needed.

**Converting weight to fuel and CO₂**
- IATA: each extra tonne carried burns about 30 kg of fuel per flight hour (about 0.03 kg per kg per hour) [IATA Knowledge Hub](https://iata.org/en/publications/newsletters/iata-knowledge-hub/fuel-efficiency-in-aviation-why-it-matters-more-than-ever "each extra tonne burns about 30 kg per hour."). The industry rule of thumb is 3% per hour [AircraftIT](https://www.aircraftit.com/articles/a-fuel-efficiency-masterclass-part-two-of-three/ "the number used for a rule-of-thumb calculation is 3% per hour").
- A model cited by Airbus: for an aircraft flying 1,825 flights a year, removing 1 kg of unnecessary load saves about US$4,210 a year in fuel. Including logistics, each pound of catering weight costs about US$10,000 a year [Aeronautics Magazine](https://aeronauticsmagazine.com/featured/airlines-could-save-millions-per-year-by-letting-ai-decide-how-much-food-to-load-airbus-says "eliminating just 1 kg (2.2 lb) of unnecessary load across 1,825 annual flights saves approximately $4,210 per year in direct fuel costs.").
- Worked example (our estimate, not from a source): carrying 10 kg less food on a roughly 13-hour HKG–LHR flight saves about 3.9 kg of fuel; at about 3.16 kg CO₂ per kg of fuel, that is about 12 kg less CO₂.

## 2. Airline and supplier case studies

- **KLM (TRAYS AI):** predicts no-shows and caters for the expected number of boarding passengers. A three-month analysis showed 63% less food wasted than catering for every booked passenger [Onboard Hospitality](https://www.onboardhospitality.com/klm-combats-food-waste-with-ai/ "A three-month analysis shows that 63% less food is wasted compared to catering for every booked passenger."). On intercontinental flights from Schiphol, that is 2.5 fewer meals (1.3 kg) wasted per flight and 111,000 kg a year [AviationA2Z](https://aviationa2z.com/index.php/2024/02/06/klm-using-artificial-intelligence-to-reduce-food-waste/ "a decrease of 2.5 meals (1.3 kg) wasted per flight. This results in an annual saving of 111,000 kg of meals").
- **Lufthansa Group:** passengers on short- and medium-haul flights can pre-order meals; fresh products on the last flight of the day are sold at a discount as "to go" [Lufthansa Group](https://business.lufthansagroup.com/gb/en/news/lhg-ai-reduce-food-waste-2025 "can pre-order their preferred meal and use the 'to go' offer to purchase all fresh products at a reduced price on the last flight of the day."). It also uses AI image recognition to see whether each meal was partly eaten, fully eaten or untouched, analysed by route and cabin, to optimise portions and menus [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Airline-News/AI-solutions-waste-reduction-airline-catering "AI recognizes whether each meal has been partially eaten, completely eaten or left untouched. The route and cabin class are also included in the analysis."). The sources give no quantified waste reduction.
- **Emirates:** Business Class passengers can pre-select their main course from 14 days to 24 hours before departure, partly to reduce waste [PAX International](https://www.pax-intl.com/assets/pdf/passenger-services/catering/emirates-now-offers-inflight-meal-preordering-service-across-92-destinations.pdf "preselect their main course between 14 days and 24 hours in advance of their ﬂight departure, ensuring they get their preferred choice every time as well as helping to reduce food wastage."). A start-up is also suing Emirates, alleging it stole the pre-order technology [View from the Wing](https://viewfromthewing.com/startup-pitched-emirates-on-business-class-meal-preorders-new-lawsuit-says-the-airline-stole-the-tech/ "Emirates is being sued for stealing the technology behind passengers ordering their meals in advance of travel."). This is an intellectual-property warning for Cathay.
- **JAL:** launched the "Ethical Choice – Meal Skip Option" in December 2020, letting passengers tell the airline in advance that they don't want a meal [Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/japan-airlines-plane-food-fertilizer/ "In December 2020, it introduced the Ethical Choice – Meal Skip Option, which encouraged passengers to inform the carrier in advance if they wanted to forego a meal."). It was extended to all international routes from December 2022 [JAL Press](https://press.jal.co.jp/en/items/uploads/EN%29Meal%20Skip.pdf "will be available on all international routes from December 2022.").
- **Airbus Smart Catering** (shown at AIX 2026): needs no new hardware. Crew tablet cameras recognise trays and scan drink codes; data goes to the cloud, where ML models it by route, cabin and season. Trials with Virgin Atlantic in 2025 on LHR–JFK (A330) and LHR–MCO (A350) target a double-digit percentage cut in preventable waste. Airbus says that if a fleet carries 3,200 lb less catering weight on average, MTOW falls 2% and fuel costs fall 1.5% [Aeronautics Magazine](https://aeronauticsmagazine.com/featured/airlines-could-save-millions-per-year-by-letting-ai-decide-how-much-food-to-load-airbus-says "For a fleet averaging a 3,200-lb reduction in catering deadweight, the result is a 2% reduction in maximum takeoff weight and a 1.5% cut in fuel costs.").
- **Winnow** (kitchen AI, usable in airline catering kitchens): across more than 3,500 kitchens, clients halved food waste on average and cut food costs by 2–8% [Winnow](https://www.winnowsolutions.com/ "Across 3,500+ locations in 90+ countries, clients cut food waste by half and food costs by 2 to 8%."). First-year returns are typically 2–10× [Winnow Buyer's Guide](https://www.winnowsolutions.com/resources/news/food-waste-management-buyers-guide "helps clients cut food costs 2 to 8 percent with a typical 2 to 10x first-year return").
- **Not yet verified:** quantified AI results from Singapore Airlines (Book the Cook), Qantas, ANA, Air New Zealand, Etihad, easyJet/Ryanair and gategroup/LSG/dnata. This search found no sources with numbers; further searching is needed.

## 3. Regulation: why forecasting beats recycling

- EU Regulation 1069/2009 classifies international catering waste (ICW) as Category 1 material that must be incinerated or deep-buried in an authorised landfill [IATA/A4E Joint Statement](https://www-int.iata.org/contentassets/821b593dd8cd4f4aa33b63ab9e35368b/joint-statement-towards-smarter-regulation.pdf "The current EU legislation (Regulation (EU) 1069/2009) requires that ICW is subject to strict control and treatment by incineration or disposal by deep burial in an authorized landfill.").
- The UK likewise classifies ICW as a high-risk Category 1 animal by-product [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/handling-and-disposing-of-international-catering-waste "ICW is classed as a high-risk category 1 animal by-product (ABP).").
- IATA says ICW legislation is the main barrier to reuse and recycling [IATA Fact Sheet](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-cabin-waste/ "A major barrier to greater reuse and recycling of cabin waste is International Catering Waste (ICW) legislation adopted by many governments"). Trade media also point out that under these rules, cutting the load in advance is the only practical way to reduce this waste stream [Aeronautics Magazine](https://aeronauticsmagazine.com/featured/airlines-could-save-millions-per-year-by-letting-ai-decide-how-much-food-to-load-airbus-says "making preemptive load reduction the only practical means of cutting the waste stream.").
- Australian biosecurity rules: the original text was not found in this search.

## Summary
- Benchmarks: 0.94 kg of waste per passenger (2023 ASF audits) or 1.43 kg (older measure); 3.6 million tonnes a year; 18–34% untouched; about US$4–6 billion incinerated or landfilled each year.
- The strongest quantified case is KLM TRAYS: 63% less waste than catering for all booked passengers, 111 t a year. It works by forecasting no-shows, which maps directly onto the Cathay concept.
- Pre-ordering (Emirates, Lufthansa) and meal-skip options (JAL) are now common, but almost no quantified results are published. That is where Cathay can differentiate.
- Airbus and Lufthansa use computer vision to capture what passengers actually eat, which could feed a forecasting model.
- Fuel conversion: about 30 kg of fuel per tonne per hour; about US$4,210 per kg per year.
- Because ICW rules require incineration or burial, recycling has little room to help. Forecasting at the source and loading less is the key lever.
